## 2026-09-14 — GEN-716 Phase 1 grounding-flag: approved design rested on two reasoning failures, both caught by the Step-1 /check (Layer 5)

**Context.** GEN-716 Phase 1. A prior session (2026-09-14) formed the "grounding flag" design — replace `stop-claim-linter.js` Phase 1 (lexical `CLAIM_PATTERNS` + evidence-marker detector) with a check that nudges high-stakes factual claims not grounded in the session's tool-call evidence, at "📌 For you" block-emit time — and queued `/vet-code` Step 1 (`/check`). Erez approved building after Phase 0. This session ran that `/check` at max effort (3 independent Opus reviewers: pre-mortem, holistic, soundness). All three returned REVISE and converged independently on the same two failures. I then verified both against the live code and Phase 0 data myself (a reviewer relay does not meet the verify-before-asserting bar).

**What happened — two independent reasoning failures in the approved design:**

1. **Architectural: the design's core "reuse the injection channel" premise is contradicted by the host file's own documented behavior.** The design's central mechanism is "reuse the file's `additionalContext` injection channel to nudge the block." But that same file's code + comments (verified this session) establish the reused channel deliberately NEVER scans the block-carrying message: the watchdog is cleared at L823; the Phase-2 guard exits at L873–937; the Phase-1 skip exits at L951–954; `findNakedClaims` (the detector being replaced) runs only at L964 — after both block exits. Comment L916–918: "Quality nudging of the block now rides ONLY the non-blocking pre-block channels … which inject into the continuation BEFORE the block is written." So replacing Phase 1 cannot deliver "correct claims in the block at block-emit time" — the detector structurally cannot see its targets. The design cited this file's GEN-467 duplicate-block reasoning but missed that the very same mechanism means the channel can't see the block.

2. **Evidence conflation: the justification was presented with confidence but the mechanism can't act on most of the cited instances.** The handoff WHY stated "the catchable errors cluster in 3–4 mechanical shapes → the flag targets exactly those," presenting Phase 0's 4%-wrong finding as the flag's justification. But of the 4 measured wrong blocks — B043 & B076 (internal-inconsistency miscounts), B006 (misread of present evidence), B015 (premature panel result) — only B015 is catchable by a groundedness check; 3 of 4 are grounded-but-wrong, a class the design explicitly excludes. The flag's real target (ungrounded "unverifiable" claims) had its wrong-rate explicitly NOT measured. So the build was justified by data the mechanism largely cannot act on, and the "targets exactly those" claim conflated "the measured 4" with "what the flag catches."

**Why it matters.** Both are "wrong design conclusion delivered with confidence" that the design-forming session's self-review did not catch. Had the build proceeded to Step 2, the architectural miss would have compounded across implementation — a detector built that cannot see its input. This is the same build-before-measuring-the-target-rate gap that retired GEN-450. It was caught only because `/vet-code` correctly gates Step 2 behind a Step-1 `/check` (Layer 5 working as designed).

**Lesson / candidate mechanism.** (a) When a design's core move is "reuse an existing channel/component," verify against that component's own documented behavior that it actually operates where the design needs it — before treating the reuse as settled. (b) When citing a measurement as justification, map each measured instance to catch/miss under the proposed mechanism; "the measurement exists" is not "the mechanism acts on it."
